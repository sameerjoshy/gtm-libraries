# Creator deal structure

## Purpose

A creator fee has to be structured so the risk sits with the party that can carry it. A flat fee puts the risk of weak results on the buyer. A per-click fee puts the risk of a weak post on the creator. This skill chooses between the two and sets the terms so both are fair.

## When to use

- A creator asks for a fixed fee, and the team needs to decide whether to agree.
- A campaign needs a performance element.
- A new creator deal is being priced and the structure is open.

Route elsewhere when:

- The question is what the creator's rate should be. Use `creator-rate-band`.
- The question is the reply to a counter-offer. Use `creator-negotiation`.
- The question is measuring the return. Use `creator-programme-measurement`.

## Inputs

- The creator's proposed fee, and the expected number of clicks from the post.
- The campaign goal: a message, or qualified traffic.
- The definition of a qualified click, and the tracking that will count it.
- The unit economics: the cost per click and deal value the structure has to work within.

## The rule

**Pay flat when you are buying a message. Pay per click when you are buying pipeline.**

- **Flat fee** suits a launch, a narrative, or a brand moment where control of the message matters more than clicks.
- **Per-click fee** suits a campaign whose goal is qualified traffic, where the team can measure the result and wants weak posts to cost little.

## Why the structure matters

The same flat fee can produce very different cost per click depending on how the post lands. If the post produces many qualified clicks, the cost per click is low. If it produces few, the cost per click is high, and the buyer absorbs the difference.

Under a per-click fee, the cost per click stays near the agreed rate. A weak post costs little because few clicks are paid for. A strong post can scale without renegotiation.

A flat fee caps the upside for the creator. A per-click fee caps the downside for the buyer, who pays only for clicks that count.

## Defining a qualified click

A performance deal is fair only if "qualified" is defined in writing. A workable definition:
- the click is tracked to the company's site,
- the visitor stays a minimum time, set by the team (a common default is thirty seconds),
- duplicate and bot traffic are excluded.

Agree the definition before the campaign starts, and state how disputes are resolved.

## Unit-economics check

For either structure, check the result against the breakeven cost per lead the team already uses:

1. Estimate the number of clicks the post is likely to produce, using the creator's past performance.
2. Compute the cost per qualified click under each structure.
3. Compare the cost per qualified click with the breakeven, using the lead-to-won conversion rate.
4. Choose the structure that keeps the cost per qualified click under the breakeven for the expected range of outcomes, not only the expected case.

## Procedure

1. **Decide the goal:** a message, or pipeline.
2. **Choose the structure** using the rule above.
3. **If performance-based,** write the qualified-click definition and the dispute process.
4. **Run the unit-economics check** over a low, expected, and high outcome.
5. **Agree the structure,** with the review date and the payment terms.

## Output

- The structure chosen, with the reason.
- The qualified-click definition, if performance-based.
- The unit-economics check across three outcomes.

## Quality bar

The structure matches the goal. A performance deal has a written qualified-click definition agreed before launch. The check covers a weak outcome, not only the expected one. Watch for a flat fee chosen for pipeline, per-click terms without a definition, and a deal priced on the best post.
