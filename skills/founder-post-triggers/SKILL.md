# Founder post triggers

## Purpose

A founder without launch results often thinks there is nothing to say. There is usually plenty: customer conversations, churn reasons, failed experiments and pricing decisions that already happened. This skill turns those into a list the founder can pick from, and keeps every post tied to a first-hand fact.

## When to use

- A founder has nothing to post and does not know what to write.
- A founder asks whether a personal brand is worth building before there is traction.
- A post idea is needed from this week's work.

Route elsewhere when:

- The question is what happens after a post is published. Use `post-launch-window`.
- The question is a company newsletter. Use `newsletter-issue-formats`.

## Inputs

- The founder's recent activity: customer calls, support questions, churn reasons, experiments, pricing changes, and product decisions.
- The one topic the founder will hold most posts inside.
- The audience the founder serves, in their own words.

## The triggers

**Customer evidence**
1. The surprising sentence: a customer line that changed your view. Quote it exactly.
2. The real no: the actual reason a prospect declined, not the polite one.
3. The exit reason: why someone left, and your read of it.
4. The recurring question: one asked three times in support. Answer it in public.
5. Their words, not yours: how a customer described you to someone else.
6. The unintended use: someone using the product for something it was not designed for.

**Numbers you can stand behind**
7. Before and after: one conversion rate, one change, both figures.
8. The retention read: a churn figure and your honest diagnosis.
9. Channel cost direction: what a channel costs, and whether that is rising.
10. The failed experiment: what you tried, what it returned, and what you concluded.
11. The expectation gap: the forecast against what actually landed.

**Decisions and reversals**
12. What you cut: a feature removed, and what removing it cost.
13. The reversal: a decision your team argued you out of.
14. The thing nobody used: built, shipped, and ignored.
15. The pricing change: what moved, and why.
16. The deliberate no: a customer, segment or feature you turned down.

**The product as demonstration**
17. Raw output: what it just produced, unedited.
18. Before and after: the same task with and without it.
19. The hard part: a difficult problem and your solution.
20. What it refuses to do: a constraint you built on purpose.

**Position and category**
21. The real comparison set: who buyers actually weigh you against.
22. The disagreed belief: a received view you think is wrong.
23. The loose term, defined: a vague word, made precise.
24. The unpriced change: a market shift most people have not yet priced in.

## Procedure

1. **Read the founder's recent activity** and pick the triggers that match it.
2. **Choose one play and hold it.** Four plays suit different aims: publish real numbers (triggers 7 to 11); content on an audience's pain (triggers 1 to 6 and 24); showing the product at work (triggers 17 to 20); aligning position (triggers 21 to 23). Plays combine badly, so pick one.
3. **Hold most output inside one topic.** As a default, about seventy percent.
4. **Write the opening line** from a stock form, then add one sentence of real judgement, such as "this matches what we see with our own users" or "the part everyone skips".
5. **Run the pre-publish check:**
   - it contains one first-hand fact: a quote, a number or a reversal;
   - someone who read the same articles could not have written it;
   - it sits inside the chosen topic;
   - it describes a process, not only an opinion;
   - every number in it can be defended unedited.

## Output

- The shortlist of triggers that match the founder's recent work, with the play chosen.
- The draft post or the opening line.
- The pre-publish result.

## Quality bar

Each post is anchored in a first-hand event. Numbers are defensible unedited. The chosen play is held. Watch for a draft with no first-hand fact, an announcement that says what shipped and not what it means, and waiting for results before starting.

## Rules

- Anchor every post in a first-hand event from the work.
- Hold most output inside one topic.
- Never publish a number that cannot be defended, and never dress up a weak one.
- Do not wait for results before starting.
