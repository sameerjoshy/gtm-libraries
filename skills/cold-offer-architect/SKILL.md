# Cold offer architect

## Purpose

When cold outreach fails, the offer is usually the problem and the copy is blamed. An opener asks a stranger for interest in exchange for nothing they can value. An offer is a complete deal: a specific outcome they already want, a mechanism that makes it credible, the risk moved off the buyer, and clear terms. This skill builds the deal first and then tests it, because copy written before the deal exists produces polite openers.

## When to use

- Cold outreach gets no replies, or no bookings, despite good writing.
- A new cold offer is being designed.
- Someone says "we changed the copy and nothing happened".
- An existing offer needs a pass or fail check before it ships.

Route elsewhere when:

- The copy and sequence come after the offer. Use `outbound-cold-email`.
- The diagnosis of a live campaign is needed. Use `outbound-performance-diagnosis`.
- The audience has not been chosen. Use `icp-builder` or `prospect-list-building`.

## Inputs

- The audience: role, segment, and their current cost of the problem.
- The outcome the audience already wants, in their words.
- The evidence that the mechanism works, and any proof with permission to use it.
- What the seller can afford to put at risk, and what the buyer must give up in effort.

## Procedure

1. **Architect the deal** before any message. State the outcome, the mechanism, the risk the seller carries, the effort required from the buyer, the proof, and the reason the offer is this generous.
2. **Score all eight criteria**, pass or fail, with no averaging:
   - Specific, quantified, time-bound outcome. A "no" is "more pipeline" with no number or clock.
   - Believable mechanism. A sceptic can see why this works, and why this seller.
   - Risk sits on the seller. The buyer does not spend money, time, or reputation to find out.
   - Near-zero effort for the prospect. A meeting before any value lands counts as effort, and fails.
   - A stated reason why the offer is this good. Unexplained generosity reads as a trick.
   - Proof embedded in the offer. Deferring it to "happy to send a case study" fails.
   - Leads with the buyer. A first line that opens with "we are" or "we do" fails.
   - Uncomfortable to make. If the seller is fully comfortable with it, it is probably too small.
3. **Fix the lowest-scoring criterion** and re-score. Repeat until all eight pass.
4. **Only then write the message**, as the vehicle for the offer.
5. **Diagnose a dead offer** when it reads well but gets no response. The usual failures are criteria 1, 3, and 8 failing together, which produces the soft "want it?" opener.

## Rules applied to the proof point

Every offer must contain a specific proof point: a real number, or a named comparable where the comparable has agreed to be named. Do not invent a number to pass the first criterion. Do not promise an outcome that cannot be defended.

## Output

- The architected offer: outcome, mechanism, risk, effort, proof, and rationale.
- A pass or fail table for the eight criteria, with the fix applied to each failed one.
- The final score, and the weakest criterion if any remains.
- The message draft, only after the offer passes.

## Quality bar

Every criterion has been scored, none averaged. The offer has a real proof point. Value is given before any meeting is requested. Watch for openers dressed as offers, proof deferred to later, invented numbers, and offers that a fitting prospect could comfortably ignore.
