# Board meeting prep

## Purpose

A board can only use a meeting for judgment if the context has already been read. This skill builds the material in the order that makes that possible: who is in the room, what decision is needed, what the board must believe to say yes, the evidence for each belief, and a discussion plan. Slides come last.

## When to use

- A board, investor, or executive committee meeting is coming up and needs a decision.
- A new leader is presenting go-to-market performance for the first time.
- Directors keep asking why growth is not faster, and the answer has not been framed.

If no decision is needed, the material is a report. Send it as a memo, not a board presentation.

Route elsewhere when:

- The question is the revenue model or forecast. Use `revenue-metrics` or `revops-forecasting`.
- The question is the company's internal operating cadence. Use `revenue-operating-cadence`.

## Inputs

- The meeting date, the attendees, and the directors' known interests.
- The decision or decisions the room must make.
- The performance data for the period, and the standing report format if one exists.
- Any capital request, with its cost, the expected return, and what has already been proven.

## Procedure

1. **Name the audience.** For each director, write the lens they will apply (benchmarking, capital efficiency, execution, governance) and the question they are most likely to ask.
2. **Write the primary decision.** One decision. Add up to two secondary asks for advice or input. If there is no decision, send a memo instead.
3. **State the story.** Write what the board must believe to say yes, in three governing claims at most. Each is a claim, not a topic.
4. **Place the evidence under each claim.** One conclusion per exhibit, stated in its title. Deep detail goes to an appendix, to be shown only if a director asks.
5. **Run the altitude check.** Restate every finding at the level of the company. A board asks what limits growth, what sequence removes it, and where the board can help. A report on one function is a correct answer to a smaller question. Rewrite it as the company constraint, the cross-functional sequence that addresses it, and the action only the board can take.
6. **Frame any capital request as a comparison.** Show the chain from investment to result, what has been proven at a smaller scale, the milestone that releases the next tranche, and the cost of delay.
7. **Design the room.** Retrospective, then orientation, then the decision. Plan the time at about 25 percent presentation and 75 percent discussion.
8. **Close on a named decision:** who, what, by when, and what it unlocks. Record it in writing within one business day.
9. **Send the pre-read** seven calendar days ahead, and never fewer than five business days ahead. Directors read it, so the meeting itself is for judgment.

## Output

- Director map with lenses and likely questions.
- The decision brief: primary decision, secondary asks, the claims, and the evidence under each.
- The pre-read, written so it can stand alone.
- The room plan with timings.
- A list of likely director questions and the answer to each.

## Quality bar

One primary decision is stated in the first two minutes. Findings are at company altitude. Capital requests are compared, not stood alone. The pre-read goes out on time. The meeting ends with a recorded decision with an owner and a date. Watch for a pre-read presented aloud, forecasts defended instead of causal chains, and no decision named at the end.
