# Creator negotiation

## Purpose

A creator who pushes back is telling the team something about price, scope, or fit. A reply that flinches gives away money and sets a precedent. A reply that ignores the pushback loses the creator. This skill gives a considered response that holds a position, with options the team can choose between.

## When to use

- A creator has countered an offer with a higher rate or different terms.
- A creator has pushed back on deliverables, timing, or usage rights.
- A deal is stalling and the team needs a response that keeps it open.

Route elsewhere when:

- The team has no rate band yet. Use `creator-rate-band` first.
- The question is the payment structure. Use `creator-deal-structure`.

## Inputs

- The creator's message, pasted in full.
- The team's original offer: rate, deliverables, timing, and terms.
- The rate band for the creator: initial, budget, and maximum.
- How much the team wants this creator, and what the campaign can absorb.

## Procedure

1. **Read the pushback.** Classify it as a price demand, a scope request, a timing change, a usage-rights request, or a combination.
2. **Check the position** against the rate band. If the creator's demand sits within the budget figure, the team has room to hold. If it sits above the maximum, the team needs a reshape or a decline.
3. **Write three options, ranked:**
   - **Hold:** keep the offer and explain the value behind it, with the rate band as the basis. Use when the offer is already fair.
   - **Reshape:** keep the total within the band but change the mix: fewer deliverables, a different format, a longer timeline, or a performance element.
   - **Accept with conditions:** agree to the higher figure only in exchange for a stated condition, such as a shorter usage-rights period, exclusivity limited to the category, or a fixed publishing date.
4. **Trade every concession** for something in return. Do not give a rate increase for nothing.
5. **Write each option as a ready-to-send draft** that is short, specific, and leaves a door open.
6. **Name the walk-away point.** State the figure above which the team will not go, so the decision is made before the reply is sent.

## Output

- The classification of the pushback.
- Three ranked options, each with its reasoning, the trade offered, and the draft reply.
- The walk-away point.

## Quality bar

Every concession is traded. Each option stays inside the rate band or states why it does not. Drafts are short and do not apologise for the offer. The walk-away point is set before the reply is sent. Watch for a rate raised without a trade, options that all say the same thing, and a deal agreed above the maximum.
