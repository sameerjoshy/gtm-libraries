# Newsletter positioning

## Purpose

Most newsletters fail at launch because nobody can say what they are for. The content may be good, but a reader scanning an inbox cannot tell what they gain. This skill makes the positioning explicit before the first issue: who it is for, what it changes for them, and what it is called.

## When to use

- A newsletter or content series is being launched.
- An existing newsletter has slow growth and no clear angle.
- A company is renaming a newsletter or merging two.

Route elsewhere when:

- The question is the welcome emails new subscribers receive. Use `newsletter-subscriber-onboarding`.
- The question is the format of each issue. Use `newsletter-issue-formats`.
- The question is the subject line of one email. Use `email-subject-and-copy-review`.

## Inputs

- The intended reader, in role and situation.
- The problem the reader is trying to solve, in their own words if available.
- What the owner knows that the reader does not.
- How often the newsletter will publish, and who writes it.

## Procedure

1. **Interview for the reader.** Ask who reads it, what they are stuck on this week, and what they would lose if the newsletter stopped. Do not write the proposition until the reader is specific.
2. **Interview for the difference.** Ask what the owner says that others in the space do not, and why the owner is credible to say it.
3. **Write the value proposition** in one sentence that names the reader, the outcome, and the cadence. A proposition that could describe any newsletter in the category has failed.
4. **Generate names** across at least three distinct strategic categories, such as names that describe the outcome, names that carry the owner's voice, and names that signal the format. Give at least ten options in total.
5. **Test each name** against the proposition. A good name is easy to say, does not need explaining, and does not collide with a known brand in the category. Check for collisions before recommending a name.
6. **Recommend two or three names** with the reason for each, and let the owner choose.

## Output

- The value proposition, in one sentence.
- Name options in three categories, with the top two or three recommended and why.
- A list of names dropped for collision or confusion, with the reason.

## Quality bar

The proposition names a specific reader and outcome. Names are generated in distinct categories, not variations of one idea. Collisions are checked before a name is recommended. Watch for a proposition that could fit any newsletter, names that need explaining, and no check for existing brands.
