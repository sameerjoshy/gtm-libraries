# Bench strength and succession

## Purpose

A business that loses its VP Sales for a month should know, before it happens, who covers the role, how ready each cover is, and what would have to be built to make the cover permanent. This skill produces that view for the roles the business cannot afford to lose, and it treats potential as a claim that needs evidence.

## When to use

- A board or investor asks whether the team could survive a departure.
- A key-person risk is suspected, for example one rep holds most of a segment.
- A talent review is due, or a high-potential list needs updating.
- A senior role is about to open, and the question is internal or external.

Route elsewhere when:

- The question is one leader's development. Use `leadership-development-diagnostic`.
- The question is hiring one candidate. Use `sales-hire-scorecard` or `sales-hire-offer-close`.

## Inputs

- The list of roles and their holders, including the leader's own role.
- Performance evidence for each candidate: results against target, the quality of those results, and how they were produced.
- Development evidence: stretch assignments, cross-functional work, feedback from peers and managers.
- Any known retirements, flight risks, or planned promotions in the next eighteen to twenty-four months.

## Procedure

1. **Name the critical roles.** A role is critical if losing its holder would put a quarter's number, a top customer, or a key capability at risk within ninety days. Include the leader's own role. Then scan the horizon for seats that will open.
2. **Score the bench for each critical role.** Count the qualified backfills and how soon each could take the role. Mark the incumbent as a single point of failure where no backfill exists. Compute a bench score across the roles.
3. **Assess performance with evidence.** Use results against target, the quality of those results, and how they were achieved. A placement without data is not allowed.
4. **Assess potential with evidence.** Four signals: how fast the person learns from new situations, their stated aspiration, the scope of decisions they already make well, and whether the people around them get better. A claim of potential without one of these signals is a hope.
5. **Map and decide.** Place people on a performance and potential grid as a conversation tool. Then make a readiness call for each successor: ready now, ready in twelve months, ready in twenty-four months, or not a successor for this role.
6. **Run the talent review with bias controls.** Each reviewer scores independently before the group meets. Evidence is on the table before opinions are. Name the decision owner for each call.
7. **Write the plans.** For each critical role, set out:
   - interim cover,
   - the short-term backfill,
   - the permanent successor and their development plan,
   - the trigger that would start an external search.
   Readiness is built through real cross-functional work, not classroom courses.
8. **Raise the bar for outside hires.** When the plan calls for an external hire, test whether the candidate's past success transfers to this company's operating system, not only whether they were successful somewhere else.

## Output

- A bench scorecard: each critical role, its holder, the backfills with readiness, and whether a single point of failure exists.
- The performance and potential view, with the evidence behind each placement.
- A readiness call per successor, with the owner of the decision.
- The plan per critical role: interim cover, backfill, successor development, and external trigger.

## Quality bar

Every critical role has a named cover or a flagged gap. Every potential claim cites at least one of the four signals. Reviewers score before the group discusses. External hire triggers are written down. Watch for a bench score built on job titles, placements made without performance data, and a successor plan that is a list of courses.
