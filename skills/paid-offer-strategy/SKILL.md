# Paid offer strategy

## Purpose

A paid ad asks a stranger to take a step. The step is the offer. Cold B2B traffic will not sign up for a demo or hand over an email unless the offer feels worth more than the effort it takes. This skill checks the offer against that test and matches it to the stage the campaign addresses.

## When to use

- A campaign is being planned and the offer is not yet fixed.
- Campaigns get clicks but few sign-ups, and the offer is the suspect.
- A team is choosing between offers for the same audience.

Route elsewhere when:

- The question is the copy that describes the offer. Use `ad-copy-voice-of-customer`.
- The question is the stage budget. Use `paid-budget-by-stage`.
- The question is the form that collects the sign-up. Use `lead-form-quality`.

## Inputs

- The audience and the stage the campaign addresses.
- The offer under consideration, and the step it asks for.
- Click-through and sign-up rates for earlier offers, where they exist.
- A few real buyers who can test the offer before scale.

### The no-brainer test

A no-brainer offer has high perceived value and a high likelihood that the person will take it, with little sense of commitment. Test each offer on both:

- **Perceived value:** what the reader thinks they will get.
- **Likelihood and effort:** how easy it feels to say yes.

Marketers almost always overestimate how valuable their offer looks to a stranger. Test the offer with a few real buyers before scaling spend on it.

### Offer types

Every paid ad promotes an offer. Common types for B2B: a demo, a product tour, a free trial, a guide or template, a checklist, a short educational video, a webinar, an event, a calculator or assessment, and a community. The right one depends on the stage.

### Two ways to communicate value

**1. Be clear.** Answer three questions in the copy:
- Who is this for?
- What is the one big takeaway or result?
- What do I have to do, in time and effort?

Weak example: "Learn about the world of private equity from the most trusted names in finance. Sign up for the next cohort today."

Stronger example: "Founders learn how to raise and manage private equity in six weeks, one hour a day."

**2. Be compelling.** People act on pain or on desire. Name the problem or the outcome the reader wants, so the call to action feels like a next step.

Weak example: "Break into private equity with our certificate."

Stronger example: "Join founders who have raised their first round with the program's method."

Do not invent outcomes or numbers. Use only results the company can show.

### Offer strength and stage

- **Create** (building awareness and trust): low-commitment offers. Education, a guide, a short video, a community.
- **Capture** (turning interest into conversations): medium offers. A demo, a trial, a template that solves a visible problem.
- **Accelerate or activate:** offers that help a buyer decide. Customer evidence, a tailored walkthrough, a trial with a clear next step.
- **Revive:** offers that re-open a closed conversation. A new angle, a shorter trial, a guided session.
- **Expand:** offers for existing customers. Training, a review of their results, a referral route.

Match the strength of the offer to how much the reader already knows. A cold reader does not get the strongest offer on the first touch.

## Procedure

1. **Name the stage** the campaign addresses.
2. **Choose two or three candidate offers** that fit the stage.
3. **Apply the no-brainer test** to each, with a few real buyers where possible.
4. **Write the clear statement:** who it is for, the takeaway, and the effort required.
5. **Write the compelling line,** using real outcomes only.
6. **Test one offer at a time** against the same audience and copy, then keep the one that converts.

## Output

- The stage, and the candidate offers with their no-brainer assessment.
- The chosen offer, with the clear statement and the compelling line.
- The test plan that compares offers one at a time.

## Quality bar

The offer passes the no-brainer test on both value and effort. The clear statement names the audience, the takeaway, and the effort. Offer strength matches the stage. No outcome or number is invented. Watch for strong offers shown to cold readers at the first touch, offers that name a result the company cannot show, and several offers changed at once.
