# GTM operating principles

## Purpose

Execution skills (cold outreach, follow-up, research, signal handling) need a shared base: what the team believes about buyers, timing, and messaging. This skill states that base in short form, so the team judges each new tactic against the same principles.

## When to use

- Setting the outbound strategy for a segment or a quarter.
- Onboarding or training an SDR or AE team.
- Deciding how to split effort between volume and quality.
- Reviewing a team's motion when results are uneven and the cause is not yet clear.

Route elsewhere when:

- The question is the daily execution rules. Use `sdr-outbound-rules`.
- The question is one team's performance diagnosis. Use `leadership-development-diagnostic` or `outbound-performance-diagnosis`.
- The question is which signals to act on. Use `signal-detection-and-timing`.

## The six principles

1. **Copy what top performers do.** Study the best reps' sequences, calls, and habits, then turn them into a standard the rest of the team can run. Do not start from a template the best reps never used.
2. **Diagnose before prescribing.** When a team underperforms, find the cause before changing the playbook. Work through five questions in order: what behaviour is missing, is it the individual or the process, is it a skill gap, and what part does the leader play in the outcome. A coaching fix applied to a process problem wastes a quarter.
3. **Act on signals, not lists.** Outreach timed to a real buying signal converts far better than outreach to a static list. Treat the signal as the reason to reach out now, and the list as the place to look for signals.
4. **The market is not used up by one angle.** A prospect who did not respond to one angle may respond to another, later, when their situation has changed. Re-engage with a new reason, not the same message.
5. **Lead with the buyer's problem, not the product.** A useful test: could the opening line be said by a competitor? If yes, it is a feature, not a problem. Rewrite it around the problem the buyer already names or would recognise.
6. **Segment and convert; do not over-personalise.** A small set of well-built sequences for well-defined segments outperforms many hand-built sequences that no one maintains. Personalise where the segment's logic supports it, not for every contact.

## Multi-channel pattern

Channels work in sequence, not in isolation. A generic pattern:

| Channel | Role | Typical timing |
|---|---|---|
| Paid or content | Build familiarity before a direct approach | Before outreach begins |
| Email | Primary direct outreach | First touch |
| Social | Secondary touch, lighter ask | Within a few days of email |
| Phone | Follow-up after engagement | After a reply or a visit |
| Referral | Use the existing network | Throughout |

The pattern is a starting point. The team's own response data decides the order.

## Mindsets

- **On personalisation.** Personalise to the person's role and situation, not only the company. Use specific facts, not vague praise. Research should shape the approach, not only the first line.
- **On volume and quality.** Fewer signal-based messages beat many cold ones. Stack signals before deciding priority: when several independent signals arrive together, reach out the same day.
- **On timing.** Act on a site visit within a day or two. A new role in the first few weeks is a strong moment for a relevant conversation. After a funding announcement, wait a few weeks before reaching out.
- **On messaging.** Lead with the problem. Soft asks tend to get more replies than hard asks. Light humour can reduce tension, but only where it fits the buyer's culture.

## Output

When this skill is applied to a plan, the output is:

- The principle or principles the plan relies on, stated in one line each.
- Where the plan departs from a principle, the reason.
- The channel sequence for the segment, with the timing used.

## Quality bar

Each tactic is traceable to one of the six principles. Timing windows and thresholds are labelled as defaults until calibrated against the team's data. Watch for a plan that personalises every contact by default, a volume target set without a quality measure, and a fix proposed before the diagnosis.
