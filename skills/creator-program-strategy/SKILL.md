# Creator program strategy

## Purpose

Creator marketing for B2B fails when it is chosen for reach and judged on likes. The right creator is the one whose audience contains the buyer, at a cost that makes sense per qualified lead. This skill sets the strategy before any creator is contacted: who the audience is, which tier of creator reaches it, how the budget is split, and what the brief asks for.

## When to use

- A team is deciding whether creator marketing fits its motion.
- A budget for creators needs splitting across tiers or creators.
- A first creator program is being designed.
- An existing program is producing content but no pipeline.

Route elsewhere when:
- The question is finding and vetting individual creators. Use `creator-discovery-and-vetting`.
- The question is what to pay. Use `creator-deal-structure` or `creator-rate-band`.
- The question is how to measure the return. Use `creator-program-measurement`.

## Inputs

- The ICP, its job titles, and the buying group.
- The average deal size and sales cycle length.
- Any existing creator or advocate relationships.
- The total budget for the test period.

## Procedure

### The tier trade-off

Creator tiers trade reach against trust and fit:

- **Nano creators** have the highest trust and the least reach. They rarely move pipeline on their own.
- **Micro creators** are cheap and trusted, but their audiences are often too small to reach enough buyers.
- **Mid-tier expert creators** tend to balance credibility, engagement, and cost per qualified lead. For B2B, this is usually the starting point.
- **Large creators** have the widest reach, the highest cost, and the weakest fit unless their audience is closely matched to the ICP.

The right tier is the one whose audience is the ICP. Follower count is secondary.

### Sequence

1. **Define the audience** by role, seniority, and topic. Write it as the buyer and the people who influence the buyer.
2. **Choose the tier** that puts that audience in front of a credible voice at an acceptable cost. Start with a small number of mid-tier creators.
3. **Allocate the budget** across a few creators rather than many. A small number of creators can be measured and managed. A large number cannot.
4. **Reserve a share** of the budget for testing one new creator or format per period.
5. **Design the brief,** with the message, the proof points allowed, the call to action, and the disclosure required.
6. **Set the review date** and the measure for each creator, before the first post.

## Output

- The audience definition and the buyer roles it covers.
- The recommended tier, with the reason, and the number of creators to start with.
- The budget split, with a test reserve.
- The review date and the measure for each creator.

## Quality bar

The audience is defined by buyer role before any tier is chosen. The start is small and measurable. A test reserve exists. Follower count does not decide the tier. Watch for a large creator chosen for reach alone, a budget spread thinly over many creators, and no measure set before the first post.
