# Newsletter subscriber onboarding

## Purpose

The first week after someone subscribes is the period when they are most likely to read, click, and buy. A newsletter that wastes it with a single generic thank-you loses the reader. This skill sets a five-email welcome sequence, with a clear job for each email, so a new subscriber learns what the newsletter is for and why it is worth their time.

## When to use

- A newsletter has no welcome sequence, or the only welcome is one automated thank-you.
- A lead magnet is being added, and the subscriber needs an onboarding path.
- The newsletter is converting few subscribers into customers.

Route elsewhere when:

- The question is what the newsletter is about. Use `newsletter-positioning`.
- The question is the regular issue format. Use `newsletter-issue-formats`.
- The question is messaging to paying customers. Use `saas-lifecycle-programs`.

## Inputs

- The newsletter's proposition and its reader.
- Three to five of the owner's best past pieces.
- A common mistake the reader makes in the area, from the owner's experience.
- The origin story: why the owner writes this, in their own words.
- The offer, if one exists, and whether it is useful without buying.

## The five emails

1. **Warm welcome.** Confirm the subscription, say what to expect, and state when the next issue arrives. Keep it short and personal. Offer a lead magnet or welcome resource here if one exists.
2. **Greatest hits.** Send the owner's best past pieces, chosen for the reader's likely problem, not for the owner's pride. Explain in one line why each one is worth reading.
3. **Biggest mistake.** Name the mistake the reader is most likely to make, and what it costs. This builds trust by showing the owner understands the reader's situation.
4. **Origin story.** Tell why the owner writes this newsletter. A real reason builds more trust than a list of credentials.
5. **Soft offer.** Make one offer that is useful to a reader who has not bought anything yet. It should be a next step, not a sales push. Only include an offer that would be worth reading even if the reader never buys.

## Procedure

1. **Interview for context:** the reader's problem, the owner's origin, the common mistake, and the best past pieces.
2. **Draft the five jobs** in order, using the outline above.
3. **Write each email** against its job, using the owner's voice.
4. **Check the sequence as a whole:** the reader should learn something useful by email two and have a reason to trust the owner by email four.
5. **Set the timing:** the first email on subscription, then roughly one email a day or every other day across five to seven days.
6. **Test for the reader who opens only one email.** The first email must make sense and deliver value alone.

## Output

- The five emails, each with its job, its subject, and its body.
- The timing schedule.
- A note on which email carries the lead magnet or offer, and why.

## Quality bar

Each email has one job. The soft offer is useful without a purchase. The first email works alone. The owner's origin is stated as a real reason. Watch for a sequence that opens with a sales pitch, five emails that repeat the same point, and no check for the reader who reads only one.
